---
title: Weighing light with black holes
excerpt: Group research used spinning black holes to place new limits on the possible mass of the photon.
archive: true
---

Research by members of the group used observations of spinning black holes to constrain the possible mass of the photon. Ultralight massive bosons can trigger a “black-hole bomb”: an instability that rapidly extracts rotational energy. The existence of rapidly spinning black holes therefore rules out part of the possible mass range.

The work appeared in [Physical Review Letters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.0465) and a companion [Physical Review D paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.0773). It was covered by [Ole Miss News](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/umnews/4087/), selected for an [APS Physics Synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v5/s147), and discussed by [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/mg21528824-100-heavy-photons-are-too-light-to-be-behind-dark-matter/), [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2012-09-black-holes-photon-mass.html), and [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/09/120925142605.htm).

The resulting bound was about one hundred times tighter than the previously accepted limit.
